# Hidden View — Potential Customers and Use Cases

## 1. PR & Public-Affairs Agencies

**Who:** Companies like Edelman, Burson, Weber Shandwick, APCO, etc.

**What they do:**  
They manage media relations and public perception for their clients.

**Their problem:**  
Clients ask, “How is the media covering our campaign/issue?” Analysts may have to collect hundreds of articles and manually organize them.

**How Hidden View helps:**  
Automatically groups coverage into stories/events, showing publishers, timeline, geography, topics, and spectrum distribution.

**Value:**  
Faster client reporting and more detailed media-intelligence reports.

---

## 2. Corporations / Government-Relations Teams

**Who:** Large companies with communications, public policy, or government-relations teams.

**What they do:**  
Track how their company, products, policies, or campaigns are being discussed.

**Their problem:**  
They need to know what changed, who covered it, where it spread, and which parts of the media covered it differently.

**How Hidden View helps:**  
Provides a structured view of coverage across publishers, events, countries, topics, and time.

**Example:**  
A company announces a new energy policy → Hidden View shows which outlets covered it, how coverage evolved, and where coverage was concentrated.

---

## 3. AI Companies & Developers

**Who:** AI startups, search companies, RAG platforms, research tools, and software developers.

**What they do:**  
Build applications that need current news/web information.

**Their problem:**  
Building their own news discovery, crawling, publisher mapping, clustering, and intelligence system is expensive.

**How Hidden View helps:**  
Provides an API instead of requiring them to build all of that themselves.

**Example API use cases:**
- “Find stories about NVIDIA.”
- “Give me the publishers covering this event.”
- “What other stories are related to this event?”

**Value:**  
They pay based on API usage.

---

## 4. Research Firms, Universities & Think Tanks

**Who:** Universities, policy institutes, researchers, and media-literacy organizations.

**What they do:**  
Study politics, society, media, public opinion, and information ecosystems.

**Their problem:**  
Collecting and organizing large amounts of news data manually is difficult.

**How Hidden View helps:**  
Provides structured events, stories, publishers, topics, locations, timelines, and coverage relationships.

**Example:**  
“How did US media cover this event compared with European media?”

Hidden View can provide the structured dataset for that analysis.

---

## 5. Media Companies

**Who:** Newspapers, broadcasters, digital publishers, and media groups.

**What they do:**  
Produce and distribute news.

**Their problem:**  
They want to understand how their coverage compares with competitors and how stories are developing across the media ecosystem.

**How Hidden View helps:**
- Compare coverage across publishers.
- Find related stories/events.
- Show how coverage evolved.
- Identify under-covered topics.
- Provide Story Intelligence.

They could also use **white-label Hidden View technology** inside their own product.
